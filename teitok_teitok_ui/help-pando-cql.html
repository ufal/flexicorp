Pando CQL Search Guide

Pando queries are token queries in a CQL-style syntax: close to bracket-based CQL, extended with dependency operators. You search tokens by attributes such as form, lemma, upos, xpos, or other token fields present in the indexed corpus.

Basic Query Form

A token constraint is written between square brackets.

[form="de"]
[lemma="bez"]
[upos="NOUN"]

Put constraints next to each other to search for sequences.

[form="de"] [form="facto"]
[upos="ADJ"] [upos="NOUN"]

Regular Expressions

Use /.../ for regular expressions.

[form=/^p/]
[lemma=/struggl.*/]
[xpos=/^N/]

Examples:

  • [form=/^de/] matches tokens starting with de
  • [form=/de$/] matches tokens ending with de
  • [form=/^.{1,3}$/] matches token forms of length 1 to 3

Combining Conditions

You can combine conditions on one token with Boolean operators.

[lemma="bank" & upos="NOUN"]
[upos="NOUN" | upos="PROPN"]
[lemma!="be"]

Use parentheses to group logic when needed.

[lemma="bank" & (upos="NOUN" | upos="PROPN")]

Language And Treebank

In a corpus that holds several languages or treebanks (such as Universal Dependencies), a search covers all of them unless you restrict it. Name the language, and the treebank, in one of three ways: on the token, after the pattern with within, or as a global condition. The three queries below find the same hits.

[upos="NOUN" & text_lang="English" & text_project="EWT"]
[upos="NOUN" & text_project="EWT"] within text_lang="English"
n:[upos="NOUN"] :: n.text_lang="English" & n.text_project="EWT"
  • within takes a single condition, written without brackets: within text_lang="English". within <text_lang="English"> is not valid.
  • After ::, a condition names a labelled token: label.attribute, joined with &.
  • Treebank names are not unique across languages (EWT is also an Estonian treebank), so combine them with the language.

Dependencies

For corpora with dependency annotation, > between two tokens means that the first is the head of the second, and < the reverse. ~ joins dependents of the same head.

[upos="VERB"] > [deprel="nsubj"]
v:[upos="VERB"] > [deprel="nsubj"] ~ [deprel="obj"]

A token can also carry a condition on its dependents or head, with child, parent, descendant and ancestor:

[upos="NOUN" & child [upos="DET"]]

Gaps And Repetition

The empty token [] means any token. Repetition operators work as in CQL.

[lemma="take"] []{0,3} [lemma="place"]
[upos="ADV"]? [upos="ADJ"] [upos="NOUN"]
[upos="PROPN"]+
  • ?: optional, 0 or 1
  • *: 0 or more
  • +: 1 or more
  • {m,n}: from m to n occurrences

Common TEITOK/Pando Attributes

Available fields depend on your corpus setup, but common examples are:

  • form (surface token)
  • word (often same as form)
  • lemma
  • upos, xpos
  • deprel, deps
  • feats
  • id, tuid

Practical Examples

[form="de"]
[form="de"] [form=/^f/]
[lemma="být" & upos="AUX"]
[upos="ADP"] [upos="NOUN"]
[lemma=/^anti.*/]

Notes And Limits

  • Attribute names are corpus-specific; if a field does not match, verify it is indexed.
  • Broad regex queries can be expensive on large corpora.
  • For TEITOK integration, XML fragments are enriched after query using xidx and corpus positions.
  • If results look right but fragments are missing, check that project_root/xidx exists and matches the indexed corpus.
